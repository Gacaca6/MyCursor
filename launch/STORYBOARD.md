# Leave the mouse at home — 90-second film

## PAGE
Problem: a mouse, clicker, cable or remote is absent or inconvenient. Audience: Windows laptop users, students and presenters. Goal: try the all-features free beta. Emotion: capable, relieved, curious.

## Inventory and idea
Actual cursor icon; mint active controls; dark phone UI; paper marketing surface; Bricolage Grotesque/Instrument Sans. Core gesture: a phone touch becomes PC control. Real success moments: moving a pointer, advancing a slide, playing media, transferring a file. Do not invent user counts, speed improvements, scarcity or a release date.

Considered: a one-task classroom documentary, a physical missing-mouse joke, an editorial essay about offline tools, a fake shopping receipt for unneeded gadgets, and an invisible desk controlled only by a phone. Selected the missing-mouse opening followed by a real-UI documentary: it puts the phone you already own at the center and gives the beta offer evidence. The conventional feature-card montage was rejected.

## World
One paper stage with softly lit mint reflections, depth through phone shadows, fine deterministic grain; night lighting only when the real dark UI benefits. Centered magnified product fragments. Crisp cuts, measured Motionmaxxing softLand entries, camera pans and object handoffs. Original electronic score and three emphasized transition hits. No stock photographs or synthetic people. UI fragments, rather than whole-device thumbnails, carry the evidence.

## Sound and accessibility
No voiceover: captions carry the full story at sound-off. Original coded electronic score, with silence/dips before key offers and three impacts. Text remains legible against its stage. URL held four seconds before a 1.5-second moving brand signoff. The film is prepared for release; post it only when the beta is available.
